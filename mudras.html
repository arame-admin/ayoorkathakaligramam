Kathakali Mudras — Classical Gestural Language

In Kathakali, the hands articulate what the lips remain silent to speak. Codified in the ancient Sanskrit treatise Hastha Lakshana Deepika, 24 root hand gestures (Hasthas) form an eloquent, highly expressive sign language capable of narrating epic sagas, emotional states, celestial beings, and natural phenomena.

What are Misra Mudras (Mixed Mudras)?

In Kathakali, certain mythological archetypes, celestial deities, relationships, weapons, and cosmic natural elements cannot be depicted with a single hand gesture. They require two different Mudras executed simultaneously across both hands. There are strictly 68 Codified Mixed Mudras (മിശ്ര മുദ്രകൾ) in classical Kathakali tradition.

FUNCTIONAL GRAMMAR • സമാന മുദ്രകൾ

Samaana Mudras (Same Mudras) Explained

In Kathakali, Samaana Mudras (Same Mudras) refer to hand gestures where the identical physical formation is used to denote two or more completely divergent symbols. The distinction between the meanings is brought alive through facial expressions (Mukhaja Abhinaya), eye glances (Drishti Bhedas), breathing pauses, and the cadence of the percussion (Tala).

CANONICAL SAMAANA EXAMPLE

Karthareemukham (Scissor's Face)

The Mudra Karthareemukham (Scissor's Face) is famously codified to represent both:

Signification 1
"Time" (Chronology / Era)

Performed with an expansive, sustained horizontal movement of the wrist and an introspective, distant gaze denoting eternity or chronological passage.

Signification 2
"Near / Vicinity"

Executed with a sharp, localized flick of the index and little fingers brought close to the torso accompanied by focused, direct eye contact.

SAMAANA MUDRA EXAMPLE

Mushti (The Fist)

The clenched fist of Mushti depicts divergent concepts depending on height and tension:

Signification 1
Charioteer / Strength

Held firmly at shoulder level with chest expanded and assertive gaze.

Signification 2
Curse / Death / Mud

Inverted downwards with grim sorrow or intense wrath.

SAMAANA MUDRA EXAMPLE

Hamsapaksham (Swan's Wing)

The versatile swan wing hand conveys over 53 concepts with opposing emotional valences:

Signification 1
Sword / Wrath

Double hand slicing forward with red-hot Raudra Rasa.

Signification 2
Salutation / Moon / Calm

Single hand gently ascending with serene Shanta Rasa.

SPECIAL DERIVATION

Baana Mudra (Arrow Gesture)

Derivative of Mushti with Straightened Little Finger

In the root gesture "Mushti", when all four fingers are tightly curled into the palm, straightening the small finger (little finger / കനിഷ്ഠിക) upright creates the specialized "Baana" (ബാണ) Mudra.

Codified Significations of Baana Mudra
✦
Oppose / Resist: Used in duel confrontations and disputations.
✦
Stop!!: Forceful command commanding an enemy or chariot to halt immediately.
✦
Coming / Arrival: Signaling an imminent approach or entrance onto the scene.
✦
Future: Delineating chronological prophecy or forthcoming destiny.
✦
Piercing Arrow: Directing an arrow towards its target.
STAGE CONVENTION

Addressing Anyone (Sambodhana)

Codified Theatrical Rule of Hamsapaksham

In classical Kathakali acting pedagogy codified in the Hasthalakshana Deepika, the theatrical convention of "Addressing" (സംബോധന)—calling out to or speaking directly to any person, deity, sage, or character—is mandatorily and exclusively performed using the Mudra Hamsapaksham (ഹംസപക്ഷം).

🪔
Execution: The hand is raised gracefully forward with the palm open and fingers straight, slightly curved upward like a swan's wing, directing the focus toward the addressed character.
👁️
Eye Synchronization: The actor's eye pupils lock firmly onto the character being addressed before the hand sweeps gracefully to begin the dialogue.
📜
Universal Scope: Whether addressing a king, a consort, a rival warrior, or a celestial god, Hamsapaksham establishes the bridge of communication.
